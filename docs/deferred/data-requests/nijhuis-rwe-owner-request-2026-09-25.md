**Subject:** Data availability clarification — Nijhuis et al. 2024 ECAP-controlled SCS cohort

Dear Dr. Nijhuis,

I am evaluating whether the European multicenter ECAP-controlled SCS study can support an independent analysis of participant-linked ECAP/device measurements and clinical pain outcomes. The article states that all generated or analyzed data are included in the publication. Could you clarify whether any additional de-identified participant-level data are available for qualified researchers?

In particular, could you confirm:

1. Who controls the data and who can authorize release across the Netherlands, Germany and UK study sites (NL7889, NCT05272137 and ISRCTN27710516)?
2. Whether a request can include raw ECAP waveforms, pulse-level or visit-level neural/device features, stimulation parameters, electrode geometry, timestamps, pain scores and follow-up dates; a data dictionary or example schema would help.
3. Whether these fields share a stable pseudonymous participant key, with denominators and missingness for each measurement and time point. The article reports baseline VNRS for 148 patients and `n=236/230/254` for three device-dose metrics; please clarify the unit, population, observation windows and overlap behind these differing denominators.
4. Whether participant consent, ethics decisions and sponsor agreements permit this proposed independent secondary analysis, and what DUA, security, eligibility or fee requirements apply.
5. Whether these records overlap other EVOKE, ECAP Study or real-world publications, so results can be counted as one cohort where appropriate.

If access and governance permit, the proposed retrospective analysis would first test whether participant-linked ECAP and stimulation features improve prediction and calibration of neural recruitment on held-out participants. It would compare a human-data-only baseline with a representation initialized using connectome-constrained Drosophila dynamics, plus prespecified degree-preserving rewired-connectome controls with shared initialization, shuffled-dynamics controls and naive-synthetic controls. A separate clinical-outcome analysis would be considered only if suitable pain scores, follow-up times and participant linkage are available and secondary use is authorized. No clinical benefit or cross-species transfer is assumed. A full protocol, analysis plan and data-security details can be supplied for eligibility review. Please do not send identifiable patient information by email.

Sincerely,
