# EXP-04 participant-level statistical analysis plan (draft)

**Status: conditional draft; no dataset is selected and no analysis is authorized or run.** `research/data/human-ecap-scs-access-audit.json` (audited 2026-09-26) currently says `no_owner_confirmed_usable_dataset`; all five candidates lack owner confirmation. The fifth, ENDLESS (`10.3389/fpain.2026.1922595`; NCT07211308), is recruiting and lists IPD sharing as `UNDECIDED`; its protocol plans in-clinic ECAP JSON streams but does not provide an accessible linked participant dataset or results. This plan cannot close TODO EXP-04. It defines the input gate and analysis rules, while the endpoint, tolerances, sample/seed count, and numerical decision limits remain unset pending RES-05 and protocol lock.

## 1. Scope and endpoint boundary

This artifact addresses TODO EXP-04, the participant-level statistical analysis plan. In `scientific-contract.json`, machine EXP-04 is `scs_programming_and_evaluation`; its allowed technical targets include recruitment estimation, parameter selection, or closed-loop control. Artifact/measurement validation belongs to machine EXP-03. The TODO EXP-03 draft specifies the candidate transfer/control arms. TODO EXP-05 separately covers a clinical-outcome protocol or STOP-H3.

The planned primary estimand, once RES-05 selects a compatible authorized cohort and EXP-03 freezes the arms, is the participant-level difference in performance between the connectome-pretrained candidate and each required baseline on a locked test of unseen participants (and unseen settings/configurations when claimed). The endpoint is intentionally **not selected yet**: it must be one technical target supported by the selected data, defined before test access. A technical ECAP/SCS target does not establish pain, analgesia, or clinical benefit. Any clinical outcome is a separate TODO EXP-05 estimand and requires its own endpoint, follow-up, governance, and STOP-H3 decision.

## 2. STOP gate and input contract

### Gate 0 — before requesting, loading, or analyzing participant records

Do not select a primary cohort or access participant-level records until RES-05 records either (a) owner-confirmed technical fields and delivery plus the separate ethics/legal determinations required for this reuse, or (b) a documented RES-05 STOP decision. This RES-05 gate applies to technical and clinical analyses. A publication, registry record, aggregate table, planned repository deposit, or request route is not data access. The present audit supports neither dataset selection nor participant-level access.

STOP-H3-A separately governs clinical prediction: it applies when no legally and ethically available dataset links SCS telemetry/ECAP to a pre-specified participant-level clinical outcome at a fixed follow-up. On the current access audit, maintain an operational hold on the clinical branch while registry screening and owner responses remain outstanding; this is not a final project-level STOP decision. Do not run or claim clinical SCS-response prediction. An owner response alone cannot clear the ethics/IRB or legal/consent gates. A technical dataset could pass RES-05 while STOP-H3-A continues to block clinical prediction. STOP-H3-B rejects cross-patient generalization if the effect vanishes on participants absent from fitting.

## 3. Analysis population, split, and leakage prevention

The inferential unit is the **participant**. For technical records, pulses are nested within electrode configuration, session, and participant (ENT-05); for a clinical outcome the unit is the participant at the declared follow-up (ENT-06). Sweeps, pulses, settings, sessions, seeds, graph rewires, and repeated visits do not increase the participant sample size. Apply FT-06 and FT-07: do not count repeated observations as independent patients or reports from one trial family as independent cohorts.

1. Make a locked manifest with cohort-family, participant, session/visit, configuration/setting-cell, and observation identifiers. Keep every observation from a participant in one partition; keep overlapping reports from the same trial family together.
2. Reserve a participant-disjoint independent test set or, preferably, a separate authorized cohort. When the claim includes unseen configurations/settings, reserve those setting cells as well. Test identities and labels remain inaccessible through all model selection, preprocessing, endpoint choice, and threshold selection.
3. Use nested participant-grouped folds only for internal development when needed: outer folds group by participant; inner folds also group by participant. This estimates within-source new-participant performance but does not replace a separately held-out external test for the final claim. Ordinary pulse/waveform folds estimate new pulses from known patients and are insufficient.
4. Fit every learned transform within each training fold only: scaling/normalization, PCA, feature selection, artifact templates, clustering/outlier masks, thresholds, imputation rules, calibration maps, and early stopping. Do not adapt on unlabeled test participants.
5. Use the exact same eligible participants, input-processing manifest, test partition, and scoring implementation for all TODO EXP-03 arms. Report exclusions and all denominators by arm. Keep S765/S105 under the NCT04938245 cohort family unless an owner-provided participant map resolves overlap; do not infer that their reported samples are independent. Gmel et al. 2023 (DOI 10.3389/fnins.2023.1297814) explicitly says its frequency-effect results were published in Gmel et al. 2021 (S789) and reports recruitment of the same 20-patient study cohort. Treat those reports as one cohort family, not replication.

## 4. Frozen contrasts and estimand

After the endpoint has been chosen from the authorized dataset and before test access, register:

- target population and inclusion/exclusion rules;
- one primary technical endpoint, scale/units, per-participant aggregation rule, direction of improvement, detection floor where applicable, and smallest effect of interest if externally justified;
- candidate model and required controls from TODO EXP-03: human-only, naive-synthetic, degree-preserving rewired-connectome ensemble, and complete model; shuffled-dynamics follows its still-open operator definition. Keep optional diagnostics out of confirmatory contrasts;
- all arm training budgets, paired seed keys, graph ensemble handling, and the rule for aggregating predictions over seeds before participant-level inference;
- missingness handling, calibration definition, confidence interval method, multiplicity family, stopping/falsification rule, and code/hash manifest.

For a loss where lower is better define each paired participant contrast as `d_i = loss_baseline,i − loss_candidate,i`; for a score where higher is better define `d_i = score_candidate,i − score_baseline,i`. Freeze one orientation and aggregation rule in the registration. Report absolute arm performance as well as paired contrasts. The transfer criterion must be evaluated against every contract-required control; superiority to one weak baseline alone is insufficient. Do not use a pulse-weighted pooled score as the primary estimate, since participants with more recorded pulses would dominate it.

Participant-level inference, grouped splits, leakage prevention, paired-contrast orientation, no zero-imputation, and required comparator rules are data-independent and can be fixed in a versioned preregistration now. The endpoint, scale, numeric tolerance, clinically or technically meaningful effect, sample-size justification, and any cohort-specific resampling strata remain gated on RES-05 and the selected data dictionary. This draft is not itself a registered protocol.

## 5. Uncertainty, calibration, and multiplicity

- **Participant uncertainty:** form 95% confidence intervals by resampling participants, retaining all of each sampled participant's nested observations and paired predictions. Where participants are clustered by site or cohort and enough independent clusters exist, use a prespecified stratified/hierarchical resampling scheme. Never bootstrap pulses as if independent.
- **Optimization uncertainty:** model seeds and rewired graph draws are nuisance variation, not the inferential sample size. Pair seed keys across applicable arms; aggregate seed/graph predictions within participant using a frozen rule, and report sensitivity across seeds/graphs separately. Do not select the best seed.
- **Calibration:** distinguish physical acceptance calibration in machine EXP-03 (`ECAP-OBS-01` bounds on waveform, latency, recruitment, artifact) from predictive calibration here. For a probabilistic technical endpoint, report calibration plot, calibration intercept/slope and Brier score where defined. For continuous prediction intervals, report participant-level interval coverage and width. Compare the preselected calibration measure against human-only under the locked test rule. Do not invent acceptable numeric limits while data are unavailable.
- **Multiplicity:** one primary endpoint and a declared family of required candidate-vs-human-only, candidate-vs-naive-synthetic, and candidate-vs-degree-preserving-null contrasts. Use a predeclared hierarchical gate or family-wise adjustment (for example Holm) and state which rule before unblinding. Secondary morphology, latency, recruitment, and artifact characteristics remain descriptive/exploratory unless separately registered.
- **Missingness:** preserve no-response/below-detection-floor as an observed detection state when that is the endpoint, distinct from technical missingness. Never replace missing waveform values with zero. Report reasons and arm-wise denominators; perform sensitivity analyses only when their assumptions are stated. Do not present complete-case results as population-level if missingness may depend on response or participant.

Numeric ECAP-like physical bounds remain `null` under `ECAP-OBS-01.tolerance_protocol` until authorized human reference signals and frozen preprocessing exist. Probability-calibration criteria are also unset until the technical endpoint and cohort are fixed. Do not convert lack of significance in a small sample into equivalence or evidence of no effect.

## 6. Sample size and decision rules

No numeric sample-size target is defensible before RES-05 identifies the cohort, participant denominator, endpoint, and eligible independent test. After selection, justify sample size from an externally meaningful smallest effect and participant-level variance/precision. Estimate precision and attainable paired-effect uncertainty at the available participant count; any design simulation must use only authorized development data and must be completed before test access. Pulses, sessions, model seeds, graph samples, or repeated publications cannot substitute for participants.

If the number of independent participants cannot support the registered precision or evaluation, downgrade the result to descriptive/exploratory reporting with broad intervals, or stop. Do not claim confirmatory transfer, calibrated clinical prediction, equivalence, or external generalization. Report the achieved participant denominator, exclusions, missingness and confidence interval width.

Reject or withhold the transfer claim if the candidate fails the registered improvement rule against any required baseline; worsens the registered calibration measure; loses its effect on participant/configuration/setting holdout; depends on leakage, unmatched data or compute budgets, selected seeds, or test-driven preprocessing; or fails a required physical signal criterion. Report negative results and limits. No EXP-04 result by itself establishes pain relief or treatment efficacy.

## 7. Versioning and audit trail

Before test access, freeze and hash: RES-05 decision; protocol and endpoint registry; owner-approved dataset/data dictionary versions; pseudonymous participant/split manifest; cohort-overlap map; preprocessing and missingness code; model/observation-operator and graph versions; arm configs, optimizer budgets, seed and graph-draw keys; scoring and calibration code; runtime environment; and access/consent scope. Preserve immutable run manifests and aggregate participant-level outputs only as allowed by governance. Any protocol change after test access is a dated amendment and requires a new untouched test set for confirmatory claims.
